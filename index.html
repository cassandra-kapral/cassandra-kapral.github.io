Composites/Manufacturing

Carbon Fiber Motor Mount Tube

Machined an aluminum mandrel and hand-laid a carbon fiber motor mount tube using 6k, 3k, and unidirectional plies with epoxy resin. Iterated on the release process after a mold-release failure, isolating the issue to mandrel geometry through a successful scaled-down test. The mandrel was designed in CAD and machined from stock aluminum on a lathe to the tolerances required for the tube's inner diameter. After researching layup techniques by reaching out to other collegiate rocketry teams, we built a layup schedule combining 6k, 3k, and unidirectional carbon fiber with epoxy resin, then applied peel ply and shrink tape to compact the plies and control resin content during cure. The first layup applied cleanly and cured without visible defects, but the cured tube could not be removed from the mandrel — pointing to a release issue at the mandrel-composite interface rather than a problem with the layup itself. For the second attempt, we revised the mandrel prep to include a Mylar release layer and designed and built a new stand to support the cylindrical mandrel during layup. We validated this updated process on a scaled-down aluminum test mandrel, which released successfully, confirming the release method itself was sound. Scaling the same process to the full-size mandrel reproduced the original failure, isolating the problem to mandrel size or geometry rather than the release technique. Due to time and budget constraints, we paused further attempts for the semester. At IREC 2026, we gathered manufacturing insight from other teams' composite processes and are applying those learnings to a revised approach this semester.
